Fool me twice: Fidesz says Tisza duped again on Ukraine minority pledge

Instaread
Ukraine is set to hoodwink the Tisza government once again: it appears that neither Péter Magyar nor Anita Orbán has learned from their complete diplomatic failure in mid-summer, and now they are once again triumphantly announcing that Ukraine is ready to guarantee minority language rights — the Fidesz parliamentary group said.
Diplomacy
5. October 2026 5:15

According to a statement sent by the opposition party’s parliamentary group to MTI, Serhiy Babak, the chairman of a committee in the Ukrainian parliament, openly stated that the aim of the bill is to remove the Hungarian veto and thereby unblock two further negotiating chapter clusters. Since Hungary has still received no guarantees regarding the adoption and enforcement of the bill, despite the victory announcements, Ukraine continues to trample on the rights of the Hungarian minority — they added.

They noted that, meanwhile, President Zelenskyy has already signalled that the 45 billion euros secured by Hungarian and European taxpayers for this year is not enough, and that they are requesting the further 45 billion euros earmarked for 2027 to be released this year as well — something the European Union, together with the Tisza government, will surely be happy to pay out.

In their view, the Ukrainians have no intention whatsoever of guaranteeing the rights of Hungarians in Zakarpattia, and that “their sole aim is to wheelbarrow Hungarian and European people’s money into Ukraine as fast as possible.”

In a Facebook post on Friday, Péter Magyar welcomed the submission of the proposal on Thursday, writing that the legislation reinforces the legal guarantees for Hungarian-language education and the network of educational institutions. Foreign Minister Anita Orbán also wrote on Facebook that Ukraine’s Deputy Prime Minister, Vsevolod Chentsov, had personally informed her that a bill affecting the educational rights of national minorities would be put before the Ukrainian parliament.

The foreign minister stated that they welcome this step, which was preceded by bilateral Hungarian-Ukrainian professional consultations held over the past weeks and by consultations with representatives of the Hungarian community in Transcarpathia.

The adoption of the draft could reinforce the legal guarantees for Hungarian-language education and the network of educational institutions, she noted, adding that the Ukrainian parliament is expected to vote on the bill as early as October.

On 4 June, Anita Orbán reported that an important step forward had been achieved on the issue of the rights of the Hungarian community in Transcarpathia; following expert consultations, Ukraine had made comprehensive commitments to resolving the educational, language-use, cultural, and political rights of the Hungarian minority. She explained that under the agreement, Hungarian-language education could be strengthened once again, and the Hungarian language could be used more freely in school life, administration, school-leaving and university entrance examinations, as well as in several areas of community and political life.

He added that it is particularly important for the European Union to also put on record that Ukraine’s minority action plan must be brought into alignment with the results of the Hungarian-Ukrainian negotiations, and that implementation must be continuously monitored throughout the accession process.
