JHTA says industry, government and educators must move together to keep people at the heart of tourism’s digital future

/ Our Today

Tourism Awareness Week spotlights the need to align technology, workforce development and education as Jamaica prepares for the next generation of tourism

As Jamaica accelerates the digital transformation of its tourism industry, the Jamaica Hotel and Tourist Association (JHTA) is urging Government, industry and educators to work together to ensure that technology strengthens the people, skills and distinctly Jamaican hospitality that define the visitor experience.

The call comes as the Government of Jamaica observes Tourism Awareness Week 2026, September 27 to October 3, under the theme “Digital Agenda and Artificial Intelligence to Redesign Tourism.”

Speaking at the recent Jamaica Centre for Tourism Innovation (JCTI) Hospitality & Tourism AI Forum at the Montego Bay Convention Centre on October 1st, JHTA First Vice President Kerry-Ann Quallo-Casserly said the industry’s digital future must be shaped around its workforce, with education and training aligned to emerging employment needs.

“Technology may change the way tourism operates, but people will continue to determine what makes Jamaica special,” she said. “Government, industry and our educators therefore have to move together. We have to make sure that what we are teaching, what employers need and what the Government is planning are aligned with where tourism is going.”

Her comments come against the backdrop of the Government’s focus on worker retention and the changing requirements of tourism employment.

Minister of Tourism, the Honourable Edmund Bartlett, has acknowledged that the industry is losing workers and must create an ecosystem that improves retention while making tourism more attractive to current and prospective employees.

“There is a realisation that the tourism industry is losing workers, and so we have to look at how we can create a workforce with an ecosystem that is conducive to a higher level of retention of the tourism workers, while at the same time looking more attractive to the tourism workers,” Minister Bartlett said.

He also underscored the need to align Jamaican tourism workers with technological change and prepare them for emerging career paths and greater upward mobility.

JHTA President O’Brian Heron said the Association shares the Government’s objective of retaining skilled workers and building sustainable careers in tourism.

“The Minister has been clear about the need to build a workforce ecosystem that keeps our best people in tourism, and the JHTA shares that objective fully. Digital transformation gives us a real opportunity to do it, provided industry, Government and educators plan and invest together, with the worker at the centre of every decision,” Mr. Heron said.

Mrs. Quallo-Casserly said technology must translate into meaningful opportunities for workers, supported by professional development, recognised skills and clear career pathways.

“We cannot simply ask our talented people to stay. We must create reasons for them to stay.”

She pointed to the Jamaica Centre of Tourism Innovation (JCTI), through which more than 45,000 tourism workers have been certified, as an example of how certification can strengthen professional standards and career advancement.

“Certification and professional development are not simply about earning another qualification. They are about giving people the confidence and credentials to advance, compete and take ownership of their careers,” she said.

As tourism becomes more technology-enabled, she said workers will need to combine hospitality expertise with digital capabilities, including data literacy, artificial intelligence and technology-enabled customer service.

“The tourism professional of tomorrow may need to understand hospitality and technology; customer service and data; creativity and artificial intelligence. They will need to be adaptable, digitally capable and, perhaps most importantly, able to use technology while preserving the human connection that defines Jamaican hospitality.”

The transformation also presents opportunities for young Jamaicans beyond traditional hotel roles, including careers in technology, marketing, finance, entertainment, agriculture, transportation, sustainability and entrepreneurship.

“The tourism industry needs your ideas. It needs your energy. It needs your skills,” Mrs. Quallo-Casserly said.

She pointed to the Tourism AI Youth Forum, held during Tourism Awareness Week, as an important opportunity for students and educators to explore the role of artificial intelligence and digital technologies in the sector.

“We have to start earlier. We have to show our young people the breadth of opportunities in tourism and give them the skills, exposure and confidence to pursue them.”

While AI and data analytics can help tourism businesses improve efficiency, understand customer behaviour and make better decisions, Mrs. Quallo-Casserly stressed that technology must enhance rather than diminish the Jamaican experience.

“It cannot replace genuine Jamaican warmth. It cannot replicate the instinct of a hospitality professional who knows when a guest needs a little extra attention. It cannot reproduce the stories, humour, culture and personality that make Jamaica Jamaica.”

“Our task, therefore, is not to choose between technology and people. It is to ensure that technology makes our people even more capable.”

She urged tourism professionals to embrace continuous learning, while calling on employers to invest in their teams.

“Never stop learning,” she said. “If we want to retain talent, we must invest in talent.”

The JHTA said sustained collaboration among Government, tourism employers, educators, training institutions and workers will be essential to ensuring that Jamaica’s workforce evolves alongside the industry, creating opportunities for young people and experienced professionals while protecting the human qualities that make the destination distinctive.
