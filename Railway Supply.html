Grand Central’s Newcastle–Brighton bid, opposed by Network Rail, lines up four Class 180s

By Oleksandr Batrak

Published October 4, 2026

Grand Central has agreed to take four Class 180 trains on lease if its proposed Newcastle–Brighton service is approved, a route Network Rail has told the regulator it cannot support.

Grand Central said that, once the Office of Rail and Road (ORR) approves the route, the four five-car units would be refurbished by Arriva TrainCare at Eastleigh before carrying passengers. The units have been off lease for about three years since East Midlands Railway stood them down, although two briefly ran for Grand Central in late 2023. The agreement with rolling stock owner Angel Trains would take the Arriva-owned open access operator’s Class 180 fleet from 10 to 14 units.

What Grand Central has applied for

Grand Central filed its application with the ORR on 19 December 2025 under section 17 of the Railways Act 1993, noting that Network Rail had been unable to support it. The operator asked for firm rights for seven years, from the December 2026 timetable change to December 2033, and wanted a decision by 27 February 2026.

The rights sought cover four weekday trains each way between Newcastle and Brighton, plus shorter workings to and from Birmingham New Street. Trains would call at Durham, Darlington, Northallerton, York, Doncaster, Sheffield, Derby, Burton-on-Trent, Birmingham New Street, Warwick Parkway, Banbury, Oxford, Reading, Wokingham, Guildford, Redhill, Gatwick Airport and Haywards Heath. In public, Grand Central describes the plan as up to five trains daily each way.

The application left the choice of train open, naming Class 180 or Class 22X units depending on which would be available. It proposed diesel-only operation for the life of the rights, although Grand Central has ordered tri-mode Class 820 trains for its existing routes.

Why Network Rail does not support the bid

Network Rail set out its position in representations to the ORR dated 30 January 2026. It found none of the proposed services compliant with the May 2026 timetable. Of the 33 services, 24 would pass through congested infrastructure between Northallerton and Newcastle, and 16 of those had at least one conflict there.

The infrastructure manager also cited paths shared with CrossCountry’s Newcastle–Reading trains and with Govia Thameslink Railway and South Western Railway services in the south. It flagged performance risk at stations including York, Birmingham New Street and Gatwick Airport, and level crossings north of Oxford where it said it could not sell more access rights. Its letter concluded that the application “does not represent the best use of network capacity”.

Network Rail added that the rolling stock had not been confirmed and suggested the ORR test whether a December 2026 start was realistic, given the driver training and vehicle and route acceptance still required.

Gaps in Class 180 route clearance

Network Rail’s assessment shows Class 180 units are not cleared between Brighton and Wokingham Junction or between Leamington Spa Junction and London Road Junction, and are cleared only for empty running between Nunnery Main Line Junction and Swinton South Junction. Class 220 and Class 221 units are cleared for the whole journey.

Grand Central replied on 20 February 2026 that the Class 180 is already cleared for 65% of the proposed route and that its drivers know the line from Newcastle to about Burton-on-Trent. It said it would take on the gauging and vehicle change work, arguing that the remaining gaps “should not preclude the granting of access rights” and could instead be attached as a condition to running the service.

The operator also rejected the capacity case. It said its timetable complies with the Timetable Planning Rules against the May 2026 timetable and that the extra CrossCountry services Network Rail cited between Reading and York are not backed by access rights.

What happens next

The application is still on the ORR’s list of track access applications under consideration, alongside bids from other open access operators, and the regulator has not published a decision. Grand Central’s February letter still described the services as planned to start within the December 2026 timetable change, and the operator says the four extra units will enter service only once the route is approved.
